01 THE REAL TALK

You’re not
bad at time.

You’re carrying a lot. Classes, practice, rehearsal, homework, family, friends, sleep. It makes sense if your calendar feels like a game of Tetris.

Let’s make a plan that works with your actual week—not an imaginary one with unlimited energy.

Start with what’s on your plate ↗
01

Protect the essentials

Sleep, meals, getting to school. Put them on the calendar first; they’re part of the plan.

02

Plan in real time

A 20-minute gap is a 20-minute task—not a whole essay. Match the task to the time.

03

Leave some breathing room

Build in transitions and recovery. A schedule with no slack is a schedule waiting to break.

03 PAUSE & RESET

Make a little
room to breathe.

You don’t have to earn a break. Try one small reset between the things on your calendar, or whenever your brain feels too full.

A ONE-MINUTE PAUSE✳
Ready?

Let your breathing stay comfortable and easy. Follow along only if it feels good.

If focusing on your breath feels uncomfortable, stop and try one of the other resets.

⟲
BETWEEN ACTIVITIES · 30 SECONDS

Drop your shoulders

Unclench your jaw. Let your shoulders fall. Shake out your hands, roll your neck gently, and take a sip of water before you switch gears.

◎
WHEN YOUR MIND IS RACING

Come back to right now

Name 5 things you see, 4 things you feel, 3 things you hear, 2 things you smell, and 1 thing you taste. Take your time; you can stop whenever you want.

☼
AFTER THE LAST THING

Make a small landing ritual

Change clothes, put your instrument away, or listen to one song you love. A tiny end-of-day cue can help you shift out of go-mode.

↗
WHEN IT FEELS LIKE TOO MUCH

Tell someone in your corner

Try: “I’m feeling stretched thin. Can we look at this week together?” A trusted adult, coach, teacher, or counselor can help you find support.

04 A NOTE TO YOURSELF

You’re a person
before a schedule.

Some weeks, you’ll nail the plan. Some weeks, the plan will meet real life and need a rewrite. That’s not failure—that’s being a person with a full life.

Ask for help when you need it. Tell your coach, director, or teacher what’s going on. You deserve support, not just more pressure.
