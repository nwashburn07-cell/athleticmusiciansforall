01 THE REAL TALK

You’re not
bad at time.

You’re carrying a lot. Classes, practice, rehearsal, homework, family, friends, sleep. It makes sense if your calendar feels like a game of Tetris.

Let’s make a plan that works with your actual week—not an imaginary one with unlimited energy.

Start with what’s on your plate ↗
01

Protect the essentials

Sleep, meals, getting to school. Put them on the calendar first; they’re part of the plan.

02

Plan in real time

A 20-minute gap is a 20-minute task—not a whole essay. Match the task to the time.

03

Leave some breathing room

Build in transitions and recovery. A schedule with no slack is a schedule waiting to break.

04 A NOTE TO YOURSELF

You’re a person
before a schedule.

Some weeks, you’ll nail the plan. Some weeks, the plan will meet real life and need a rewrite. That’s not failure—that’s being a person with a full life.

Ask for help when you need it. Tell your coach, director, or teacher what’s going on. You deserve support, not just more pressure.
